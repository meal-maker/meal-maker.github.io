### [Lean 4 Proof Settles W\(2;3,21\)=416 via Neuro-Symbolic System](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1xivpue/w2321_416_a_van_der_waerden_number_conjectured_by/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A previously open van der Waerden number, W\(2;3,21\)=416, has been proved with a Lean 4 kernel-checked proof. The result means every red-blue colouring of \{1,...,416\} contains a red 3-term arithmetic progression or a blue 21-term arithmetic progression, while some colouring of \{1,...,415\} avoids both. Kullmann conjectured the value in 2010, and DeepMind&\#x27;s Formal Conjectures list still carried it as open this year. The proof was produced by Hupyy&\#x27;s neuro-symbolic Large Proof Model \(LPM\), spans 209 cases with 38,661,140 leaf formulas, and each refutation was checked by cake\_pr \(an LRAT checker verified in CakeML\) and bound by digest to its Lean term; a preprint and 13.6 GB evidence package are available on Zenodo.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/Rodriguez909 · Oct 29, 20:46

**「Background」** Van der Waerden number W\(2;3,21\) is the least n such that every red-blue colouring of \{1,...,n\} contains a red 3-term or blue 21-term arithmetic progression. Its exact value had been open, with Kullmann&\#x27;s 2010 conjecture placing it at 416. Lean 4 is a proof assistant whose kernel checks formal proofs, while LRAT is a proof format for SAT-style unsatisfiability refutations.

**「Impact」** This machine-checked result replaces the previously open status in DeepMind&\#x27;s Formal Conjectures list with a formally verified exact value, giving researchers a kernel-checked Lean 4 theorem and replayable LRAT evidence. Independent replication from the preprint and evidence package remains to be completed for full community acceptance.

### [Chained PHP2Shell and Linux Kernel XFRM Exploit Roots 25 Servers](https://dreamgroup.com/blog/chained-php2shell-exploit-roots-25-servers) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A chained exploit combining the PHP2Shell web shell with a Linux kernel vulnerability in the XFRM ESP-in-TCP subsystem reportedly rooted 25 servers, including US government servers. The attack first used PHP2Shell to compromise PHP applications and then exploited the kernel XFRM flaw to escalate privileges to root. The DreamGroup report highlights a real-world case where web-application and OS-level vulnerabilities were chained together against high-value targets. The incident underscores the risk of leaving unnecessary kernel subsystems enabled and the need to patch both application and kernel vulnerabilities promptly.

hackernews · 882542F3884314B · Oct 29, 10:42 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50286987)

**「Background」** PHP2Shell is a shorthand for a pair of PHP vulnerabilities that CISA has added to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, indicating active exploitation. The reported attack chain combined those PHP flaws with a separate Linux kernel vulnerability in the XFRM ESP-in-TCP subsystem, which is part of the kernel&\#x27;s transform framework used for IPsec processing, to gain root access. External coverage describes the chained exploit as affecting 25 servers across the United States, Japan, Taiwan, and South Korea, including systems in a US government-affiliated environment.

**「Security impact for affected systems」** For affected US government-affiliated environments and Palo Alto Networks firewall operators, this incident means their systems should be considered fully compromised and require immediate forensic review for Active Directory tampering, credential theft, and lateral movement.

**「Community Discussion」** Commenters questioned whether public cloud providers are adequately protecting against such exploits, while others highlighted Linux&\#x27;s large default kernel attack surface and suggested disabling unused subsystems like XFRM or MPTCP. Some debated the exploit&\#x27;s sophistication and identified the firewall vendor as likely Palo Alto Networks based on PAN-OS and SELinux configuration details.

**「Impact」** For developers and store operators in designated non-US regions, the program offers a $10,000-per-year path to one-click installation and automatic background updates on Android 17 QPR2+ devices, contingent on enterprise account status and passing Google&\#x27;s security scan of the full app catalog.
