### [Claude autonomously discovers novel CRISPR-like enzyme system](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-discovers-novel-enzyme-system) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Anthropic announced that its AI system Claude, using 950 agents over 21 hours, autonomously discovered a novel reverse-transcriptase-based, CRISPR-like enzyme system named array-associated reverse transcriptase \(ART\). The system was identified from more than 200,000 reverse transcriptases and is mainly found in phages, but its function remains unclear. Feng Zhang called the work an example of AI agents aiding biological discovery. The finding is preliminary and has not yet been functionally characterized.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 24, 01:11

**「Background」** CRISPR systems are adaptive immune systems found in bacteria and archaea that feature arrays of short, repeating DNA sequences; reverse transcriptases are enzymes that synthesize DNA from an RNA template and are common in bacterial and phage genomes. The newly reported system, array-associated reverse transcriptases \(ART\), was identified by a multi-agent Claude system that searched more than 200,000 reverse transcriptases, and the result is described in a non-peer-reviewed preprint as the first from Anthropic&\#x27;s new molecular biology lab.

**「Impact」** For biologists and AI researchers, this is a concrete early demonstration that multi-agent Claude systems can autonomously identify candidate enzymes, though functional characterization is still required before practical use.

### [Tokens Too Cheap to Meter](https://jyn.dev/tokens-too-cheap-to-meter/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The article analyzes how rapidly falling LLM token prices may soon make language model calls cheaper than traditional command-line tools, with implications for software engineering economics. It presents the trend as a move toward &\#x27;too cheap to meter&\#x27; compute, arguing that current cost gaps with tools like grep are narrowing and could reverse if price trends continue. The piece is an opinion/analysis rather than a report of a new model or tool, and it does not provide a concrete timeline.

hackernews · teoruiz · Sep 23, 09:21 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49813482)

**「Background」** The phrase &quot;too cheap to meter&quot; dates to a 1954 prediction that nuclear power would make electricity so inexpensive that metering would be unnecessary. Applying this to LLMs, the article notes that API pricing has fallen sharply, with at least one model charging $0.042 per million input tokens and offering free output tokens, making token costs increasingly comparable to per-invocation costs of traditional command-line tools.

**「Community Discussion」** Several commenters challenge the article&\#x27;s extrapolation, citing Stein&\#x27;s Law to argue that efficiency gains cannot continue forever and that per-call costs for high-quality models may not fall below compiled tools. Others note that the author glosses over business model viability, with massive infrastructure investments lacking proven returns, and one compares the &\#x27;too cheap to meter&\#x27; framing to unfulfilled nuclear power promises.

### [HBM Demand Pushes DRAM Per-Area Value Past Advanced Logic Chips](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/dram/dram-is-now-more-expensive-than-compute-chips-on-per-area-basis-ai-demand-drives-memory-die-value-past-leading-edge-silicon) ⭐️ 7.0/10

With AI infrastructure expansion, high-bandwidth memory \(HBM\) has become an essential component of AI chips. Due to its taller stacking processes, advanced packaging, and stricter yield control, HBM&\#x27;s per-area value has surpassed that of some leading-edge logic chips. Previously, advanced-process chips were considered the highest-value products in the semiconductor industry. Rapidly growing demand from AI accelerators for memory bandwidth and capacity is driving up HBM prices and industry status, increasing the importance of memory vendors in the AI chip supply chain.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 23, 11:39

**「Background」** High-bandwidth memory \(HBM\) stacks multiple DRAM dies vertically and uses advanced packaging and tighter yield controls to supply the high bandwidth and capacity demanded by AI accelerators. Traditionally, leading-edge logic chips such as CPUs and GPUs manufactured on the most advanced nodes commanded the highest value per silicon area, while DRAM was seen as a lower-margin commodity. The recent AI-driven surge in HBM demand has reversed that relationship for some memory dies, making DRAM economics more comparable to or even exceeding certain advanced logic chips on a per-area basis.

**「Impact」** Memory vendors gain greater economic weight in AI accelerator supply chains as HBM&\#x27;s per-area value now exceeds some advanced logic chips.
