### [DeepSeek Elastic Compute Paper Reports 380,000 Sandboxes Across 160 EPYC Nodes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22978) ⭐️ 7.0/10

DeepSeek has published a paper titled &quot;DeepSeek Elastic Compute \(DSec\)&quot; on arXiv describing a large-scale elastic compute system. The system is reported to run 380,000 concurrent sandboxes on 160 AMD EPYC-based server nodes. The submission from Hacker News highlights this technical achievement, which is notable for distributed systems and AI infrastructure. However, the available summary lacks an abstract and independent verification, so details of the architecture and performance remain limited.

hackernews · shenli3514 · Sep 26, 18:22 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49859112)

**「What is DSec?」** DeepSeek Elastic Compute \(DSec\) is a production sandbox platform described in arXiv paper 2609.22978 for supporting agentic training at scale. It exposes multiple sandbox backends—FnCall, container, microVM, and full-VM—so that AI training workloads can run isolated code execution environments with different levels of isolation and overhead. The reported setup uses AMD EPYC-based server nodes to host large numbers of concurrent sandboxes.

**「Community Discussion」** Commenters focus heavily on the paper&\#x27;s unusually large author list, with one estimating around 131 authors and others suggesting it may be an asset-protection strategy or a record for most authors. The technical claim of 380,000 concurrent sandboxes on 160 AMD EPYC nodes is called &quot;crazy stuff,&quot; and one commenter speculates the capability could enable a massive agent swarm for offensive purposes.

On September 25, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit upheld by a 2-1 vote the Pentagon&\#x27;s blacklisting of Anthropic as a national security supply chain risk, barring the company from military contracts. The majority said Pentagon concerns were reasonable because Anthropic refuses to allow its products to be used for autonomous weapons and mass surveillance. Anthropic disagreed with the ruling and is considering asking the full appeals court to rehear the case. A San Francisco federal judge had previously overturned the listing under a different law and blocked a broader government ban on Anthropic.
