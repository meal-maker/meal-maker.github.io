### [Training a 210M Text-to-Image DiT on One GPU](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1wdfmvq/training_a_210m_texttoimage_dit_from_scratch_on/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Ivan Mikhnenkov trained a 210M-parameter text-to-image diffusion transformer from scratch in 3.5 days on a single RTX PRO 6000, using 4.2M images at 256² and 400k training steps. He reports that two learned key/value slots appended to cross-attention become the dominant attention sink at mid-noise in a middle block, receiving about 90% of cross-attention mass while the EOS token drops to about 4%. The flow-matching loss moved only 0.805 to 0.754 and stayed equal between training and held-out sets, whereas held-out FID improved from 33.7 to 27.0 and FD-DINOv2 from 570 to 218, indicating loss is a health signal rather than a quality signal. The training-time timestep shift of 2.8 from the SD3/RAE rule was worth more than doubling sampling steps: 20 shifted steps gave FID 27.0, while 50 steps gave 26.6 and 20 unshifted steps gave 27.3.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/IvanMikhnenkov · Sep 11, 13:00

**「Background」** Diffusion transformers generate images by iteratively denoising latent tokens, often with cross-attention to text prompts; rectified flow trains a model to predict a velocity field that moves samples from noise to data. Attention sinks are tokens or slots that absorb a large share of attention weight, sometimes stabilizing transformers, and learned null slots are a register-token variant. FID and FD-DINOv2 are common metrics for image quality and semantic alignment, while detector-based object accuracy measures prompt-object correspondence.

**「Impact」** For practitioners training small text-to-image diffusion transformers on limited compute, this single-GPU recipe suggests that using learned null cross-attention slots and a training-time timestep shift of 2.8 can improve held-out FID and object accuracy, and that flow-matching loss should be monitored as a training-health check rather than an indicator of sample quality. However, these are single-run measurements on one 210M model and dataset mix, so robustness across scales is not established.
