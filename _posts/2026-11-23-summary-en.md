### [OpenAI Finds Clear Correlation Between Perplexity and Intelligence](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

OpenAI has published an analysis of the relationship between language model perplexity and intelligence. The article presents a clear trend showing that lower perplexity—less surprise in next-token prediction—correlates with higher model intelligence. It argues that prediction error, measured as perplexity, serves as a simple and useful benchmark for evaluating model capability. The analysis is positioned amid ongoing debates about evaluation metrics for large language models.

hackernews · acossta · Nov 22, 17:06 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50601525)

**「Background」** Perplexity, or its logarithm, measures a language model&\#x27;s average surprise when predicting the next token in a sequence; lower values indicate better compression of the training distribution. OpenAI&\#x27;s chief scientist Jakub Pachocki argues that pretraining cross-entropy closely tracks underlying model capabilities, with certain log-perplexity thresholds enabling downstream skills. The article follows recent discussions about using this metric as a shared, interpretable benchmark across developers.

**「Community Discussion」** Community comments define perplexity as next-token surprise, where worst-case perplexity equals vocabulary size and best case is 1. Some commenters express surprise that the trend holds after reinforcement learning, while others view it as expected because prediction/compression rate captures world modeling. Microsoft has reportedly joined the effort by reporting MAI-2 Base log-perplexity of 0.819 on OpenAI&\#x27;s held-out set.

### [O&\#x27;Reilly Essay: Silicon Valley&\#x27;s Shift from Abundance to Profit Machine](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/what-happened-to-silicon-valley/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

An O&\#x27;Reilly Radar essay argues that Silicon Valley has shifted from an abundance-driven innovation culture to a profit-optimizing machine that prioritizes shareholder value over broad value creation. The piece uses mechanism design, framed as reverse game theory, to ask what game society ought to be playing rather than accepting current market rules as natural. It cites Hal Varian&\#x27;s observation that only about 6% of early Google search result pages were monetized, contrasting that abundance with today&\#x27;s profit-focused behavior. The essay concludes by calling for reprogramming economic mechanisms so that human flourishing, not corporate profit, becomes the goal.

hackernews · greenyouse · Nov 22, 19:20 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50602947)

**「Background」** Silicon Valley&\#x27;s early culture emphasized building abundant, user-centric services even without immediate monetization, exemplified by search engines serving non-ad-based results. Over time, pressures such as shareholder value maximization and the low-interest-rate \(ZIRP\) environment pushed tech companies toward profit optimization and aggressive growth, leading to a shift in priorities. The essay draws on the concept of mechanism design, which asks what game rules should be designed to achieve desired outcomes, proposing a reorientation toward human flourishing rather than corporate profit.

**「Community Discussion」** Commenters largely agree with the essay&\#x27;s critique, with several emphasizing the need to redesign economic incentives rather than treat market outcomes as natural. Some add context: zhivota recalls early Google&\#x27;s 94% non-monetized search results as a sign of lost abundance, while CoolestBeans attributes tech giants&\#x27; growth to ZIRP and warns they are now cannibalizing core competencies through AI.
